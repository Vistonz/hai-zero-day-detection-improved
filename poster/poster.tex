\begin{myblock}{The problem}
A zero-day detector must flag attacks it has never seen, trained on normal
operation alone. The harder case is when the \emph{plant itself} is replaced by a
later, differently instrumented version: HAI 21.03 and 23.05 are two such versions
of the same testbed, sharing 59 tags but not the instrumentation.

\vspace{0.4cm}
\large
\textbf{How much does detection degrade from 21.03 to 23.05 --- and can an adaptive
decision layer recover what is lost?}

\vspace{0.4cm}
\normalsize
A detector that works in-domain may stop working when the baseline shifts, and no
amount of threshold tuning can repair a representation that no longer separates
attacks from normal operation.
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{Data}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{partition} & \textbf{files} & \textbf{rows} & \textbf{attacks} \\
\midrule
21.03 normal training & 3 & 921,603 & 0 \\
21.03 test & 5 & 402,005 & 50 \\
23.05 test & 2 & 284,400 & 52 \\
\midrule
all streams & 10 & 1,608,008 & 102 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.25cm}
The two versions share 59 public tags; 18 have zero variance throughout the normal
21.03 training data and are removed \emph{using source statistics only}, leaving
\textbf{41 modelling tags}. Their constant source encoding would otherwise dominate
max-fused scores after the shift.
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{Protocol: no labels, anywhere}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Attack labels are used for \textbf{evaluation only} --- never for training,
        model selection, or threshold calibration.
  \item Normalisation is fitted on chronological 21.03 training partitions; the last
        15\% supplies normal calibration scores.
  \item EVT calibrated on \textbf{source-normal} scores to an empirical target of
        \textbf{10 false alarms/day}.
  \item Three seeds (11, 23, 37). No point adjustment.
\end{itemize}
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{What we built}
Three detectors, trained on normal 21.03 data only:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{PCA} reconstruction on flattened windows.
  \item \textbf{Deep SVDD} --- a three-layer bias-free 1-D convolutional trunk into a
        latent hypersphere (13,904 parameters).
  \item \textbf{Convolutional VAE} --- the same trunk, plus Gaussian heads and a
        decoder (26,352 parameters).
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}
Windows are causal (60\,s, scored at the window end, 10\,s evaluation stride).
Per-sensor scores are fused by \textbf{maximum}, so an alarm needs one strongly
anomalous sensor. The two neural models share the trunk but not the total parameter
count, and that gap is reported rather than hidden.
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{Result 1: in-domain, at a realistic alarm budget}
At 10 false alarms per day on 21.03, all three detectors work moderately --- and
none of them works well:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{event recall} & \textbf{eTaF1} & \textbf{PR-AUC} & \textbf{Hit@3} \\
\midrule
PCA & 0.740 & 0.533 & 0.502 & 0.229 \\
Deep SVDD & \textbf{0.773} & \textbf{0.543} & 0.489 & 0.171 \\
VAE & 0.560 & 0.473 & 0.462 & \textbf{0.257} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.15cm}
A PR-AUC near 0.5 at a 1--2\% attack rate is a weak ranker, not a solved problem.
That matters for what follows: there was little to lose.
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{Result 2: the representation collapses}
\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{fig/fig_prauc.png}
\caption{PR-AUC is threshold-free, so it measures the representation alone.}
\end{figure}

\vspace{0.1cm}
Every model loses most of its ranking quality: PCA 0.50 $\to$ 0.11, Deep SVDD
0.49 $\to$ 0.09, VAE 0.46 $\to$ 0.23. VAE retains the most; PCA and Deep SVDD retain
almost nothing. This is the failure that a decision layer cannot repair.
\end{myblock}

\begin{myblock}{Result 3: high recall can be an alarm collapse}
\begin{figure}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{fig/fig_collapse.png}
\caption{Frozen arm. The calibration target was 10 alarms/day.}
\end{figure}

\vspace{0.1cm}
PCA and Deep SVDD report \textbf{event recall 1.000} on 23.05 --- because they fire
\textbf{8,297 alarms per day}, on 96\% of normal windows. A circular-shift null settles
it: a randomly time-shifted version of the same alarm series matches this recall in
every one of 200 shifts ($p = 1.00$). On 21.03 the same models beat their null at
$p < 0.001$. VAE reports the honest number: recall 0.154 at 16.4 alarms/day, still
7.7$\times$ its null. Raw recall is not a stand-alone measure of useful detection.
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{Result 4: DSPOT does not recover it}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{model} & \textbf{frozen} & \textbf{adaptive} & \textbf{gain} & \textbf{FA/day} \\
 & \multicolumn{2}{c}{\emph{23.05 event recall}} & & \emph{frozen$\to$adapt.} \\
\midrule
PCA & 1.000$^\dagger$ & 1.000$^\dagger$ & 0.000 & 8297$\to$8297 \\
Deep SVDD & 1.000$^\dagger$ & 1.000$^\dagger$ & 0.000 & 8297$\to$8297 \\
VAE & 0.154 & 0.115 & \textbf{$-$0.038} & 16.4$\to$5.5 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{0.12cm}
{\footnotesize $^\dagger$Recall 1.000 with 8,297 alarms/day is \emph{alarm collapse}:
a circular-shift null reaches 1.000 in 200 of 200 shifts ($p=1.00$). It is not
detection.}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.15cm}
For VAE, DSPOT cuts alarms from 16.4 to 5.5 per day but \emph{loses} recall
(recovery ratio $-0.095$). For the other two, nothing changes: they were already
alarmed everywhere.

\vspace{0.2cm}
\textbf{Why:} PR-AUC is \emph{identical} between the frozen and adaptive arms ---
4.873e-05 vs 9.481e-05 for the EVT risk parameter, same scores. DSPOT moves the
decision threshold; it does not touch the encoder, the PCA basis, or the anomaly
score. \textbf{Decision-layer adaptation cannot substitute for representation
quality.}
\end{myblock}\vfill

\begin{myblock}{Conclusions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Zero-day detection does not survive the plant-version shift.} Ranking
        quality falls by roughly a factor of four to five; two of three detectors
        collapse into continuous alarm.
  \item \textbf{The decision layer is not the bottleneck.} DSPOT reduces alarms where
        the representation still works and does nothing where it does not.
  \item \textbf{Report threshold-free metrics alongside event recall.} On the shifted
        domain, recall 1.000 and recall 0.154 can describe equally useless detectors.
  \item Limits: one testbed, two versions, three seeds; the source-normal calibration
        targets an empirical source rate, not a guaranteed shifted-domain budget.
\end{itemize}
\end{myblock}
